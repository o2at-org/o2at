Revision focus

Version 0.2 makes 1G SFP the default endpoint design, doubles the earlier maximum format rates and replaces unsupported claims about optical modules with explicit budgets. It retains standard Ethernet framing, adds a precise receiver-paced control model and separates transport capability from existing DAC, USB and I²S limits. This document is a proposal, not a ratified standard or a claim that compliant products already exist.

1. Executive Summary & Problem Statement

O2AT proposes a durable replacement for the consumer optical audio socket: inexpensive duplex glass fibre, a standard pluggable optical module and an openly specified audio protocol. The primary connection is a direct source-to-DAC link that requires neither an IP address nor a network administrator. The core standard is 1G SFP; the optional 10G fabric is not part of the minimum consumer replacement requirement. Its purpose is to preserve TOSLINK’s most valuable property, a non-conductive signal path, while removing the installed base’s restrictive format envelope and dependence on an embedded audio clock.

The engineering centre of gravity in v0.2 is 1G SFP. A 1.25 GBd 1000BASE-X link offers a 1.000 Gb/s MAC service after line coding. With the framing defined here, stereo PCM32 at 3,072 kHz occupies 203.981 Mb/s including a VLAN tag, Ethernet framing, preamble and interpacket gap. Stereo native DSD2048 in the explicit 48 kHz-derived extension occupies the same rate. Eight channels occupy 815.923 Mb/s. These are calculated transport budgets, not announcements of commercial playback support.

The audio samples cross an asynchronous FIFO before conversion. The DAC-side clock sets the consumption rate; packet arrival determines only when data become available. Every source obeys receiver credits; coherent sample-clock discipline may additionally control its long-term production rate. This removes packet-arrival timing from the intended conversion-clock path. It does not remove the receiver’s own clock phase noise, supply coupling, metastability risks or the consequences of a FIFO underflow.

A reference-tier receiver shall provide a specified 10 MHz reference input, an independently engineered audio clock generator, lock monitoring and controlled holdover. Entry-level O2AT remains useful with an internal oscillator and receiver feedback. Requiring an external reference for every consumer installation would obstruct the central objective of replacing TOSLINK with a simple, dependable optical connection.

1.1 What changes in v0.2

  • The baseline is 1000BASE-X and 1000BASE-SX, with 10G retained as an optional fabric and high-channel-count profile. A mechanical SFP+ cage may host a qualified 1G module when its host electronics support that rate.
  • The format namespace extends PCM to 2,822.4/3,072 kHz and raw DSD to 90.3168 MHz per channel, plus an explicitly labelled 98.304 MHz extension. Exact integer rates replace the overflowing one-byte multiplier field in the attached draft.
  • The audio header is consistently 12 bytes. PCM is big-endian, left-aligned in 32-bit containers. DSD uses chronological, most-significant-bit-first blocks. Wire version 0x2 intentionally supersedes the incompatible draft header.
  • Ethernet PAUSE is treated as link-local congestion control. End-to-end receiver credit and upstream clock feedback address sustained rate mismatch. Generic switches do not confer a deterministic latency guarantee.
  • Manufacturer comparisons distinguish documented behaviour from proprietary implementation details. MSB Pro ISL, HiFi Rose optical USB and standard Ethernet optics share components without sharing a wire protocol.

1.2 Scope, terminology and conformance

“Shall” identifies a proposed v0.2 conformance requirement; “should” identifies a recommendation; “may” identifies an option. The normalised machine-readable prefix is o2at. A device shall advertise only formats it can accept, buffer and deliver at its physical audio output. A link can carry a format that the attached conversion engine cannot reproduce, so negotiation shall reject unsupported modes before audio starts.

O2AT is a Layer-2 protocol within a single bridged Ethernet domain. Switches can bridge its Ethernet frames; ordinary IP routers do not route them. The prototype EtherType is experimental and shared with other experiments, so v0.2 is appropriate for controlled evaluation networks pending a production identifier and a completed interoperability programme. The proposal neither claims universal patent clearance nor grants rights over third-party standards or vendor IP cores.

2. History of TOSLINK and Legacy Optical Audio Interfaces

TOSLINK was developed by Toshiba during the early compact-disc era. Its introduction is commonly dated to 1983; a contemporaneous Toshiba archival source for that date was not located for this revision. The historical lineage includes EIAJ CP-1201 and consumer digital-audio interface work associated with IEC 60958. JIS F05 describes the familiar optical connector family. These are related layers of an interface ecosystem, not interchangeable names for a single physical bandwidth limit.1

The original design solved a practical problem well: it connected domestic digital equipment without an electrical conductor between the two signal ports. Its limitations arise from the combination of inexpensive optical components, the installed digital receiver population, embedded-clock framing and a narrow common capability set. The case for replacement is stronger when it acknowledges that properly implemented TOSLINK already provides galvanic isolation and can deliver error-free audio within its supported envelope.

2.1 Physical limits, line coding and the installed base

Typical consumer assemblies use a red LED near 650 nm and approximately 1 mm plastic optical fibre, commonly PMMA. The large core and high numerical aperture make coupling inexpensive, but step-index propagation admits many paths with different transit times. Modal dispersion, attenuation, bends, connector contamination and finite transmitter/receiver bandwidth can close the eye and shift threshold crossings. The degree depends on the complete link; a universal failure distance of three or five metres is not defensible.2

Toshiba’s TOTX1950A(F) illustrates why component-specific evidence matters: it specifies a 650 nm LED, JIS F05 coupling and a 10 Mbit/s NRZ operating limit. That is the rating of one transmitter under stated conditions, not an immutable ceiling imposed by every F05 connector. NRZ data-rate ratings, pulse-width distortion and the minimum pulse width required by a BMC stream must be reconciled before a valid audio-rate limit can be inferred.3

A conventional stereo IEC 60958 frame contains two 32-bit subframes. Its logical bit-period rate is 64 × Fs, while Bi-Phase Mark Coding resolves each period into half-bit intervals at 128 × Fs. At 192 kHz, those figures are 12.288 Mbit/s and a 24.576 MHz half-bit grid. BMC guarantees useful transitions but embeds timing in the same waveform as the data. Pattern-dependent edge displacement can therefore become recovered-clock jitter through a receiver’s clock-recovery transfer function.

That coupling is not proof that all optical edge jitter reaches the analogue output. A suitable PLL, asynchronous sample-rate conversion or a locally clocked elastic architecture can attenuate it, each with different requirements and consequences. Error-free bits and conversion-clock quality are separate questions. O2AT’s proposed improvement is to make the separation explicit in the transport architecture, rather than relying on undocumented receiver behaviour.

Conventional consumer TOSLINK commonly tops out at stereo 24-bit/96 or 192 kHz, depending on both endpoints and the cable. This is an installed-base interoperability ceiling. It is not a universal theorem about plastic fibre, F05 or the whole IEC 60958 family: IEC 60958-5 adds consumer enhancements for multichannel and higher-resolution applications. O2AT deliberately adopts a new common transport profile rather than attempting to infer support for those extensions from an existing optical socket.4

DoP is lossless packaging of DSD bits, not lossy conversion to PCM. In the standard arrangement, 16 DSD bits occupy a 24-bit PCM-shaped container with an eight-bit marker. The disadvantage is transport overhead and sensitivity to inappropriate PCM processing. Native o2at DSD avoids that wrapper and carries the one-bit sequence directly; it does not improve the numerical fidelity of an already bit-perfect DoP transfer.5

3. Landscape Analysis: AoIP and Proprietary Optical Links

3.1 Professional transports solve a broader problem

AES10 MADI established a useful precedent for dedicated serial multichannel audio. Its channel structure and professional installation model differ from a modern consumer replacement socket. AES67, RAVENNA and Dante address networked audio systems with multiple endpoints, stream descriptions, timing distribution and managed routing. O2AT should not dismiss those capabilities as inherently noisy or incapable of low latency; it deliberately chooses a smaller application scope.7

AES67 specifies interoperability requirements for audio over IP, including synchronisation and media transport. RAVENNA is a broader networking technology and ecosystem, while Dante has proprietary transport and management elements with AES67 interoperability available on supported products. It would be incorrect to assert that every AES67 endpoint mandates SIP, RTSP, mDNS and all other discovery mechanisms simultaneously. Their presence and roles depend on the implementation and system profile.

AVB and Milan already demonstrate standards-based Layer-2 audio with synchronisation, stream reservation and constrained network behaviour. Milan builds endpoint interoperability on the IEEE AVB family, including 802.1AS and IEEE 1722/1722.1. O2AT does not invent deterministic Ethernet. Its proposed distinction is a simpler optical consumer profile, native DSD packing, an explicit high-rate envelope and an optional external reference architecture without mandatory network time synchronisation.8

3.2 HiFi Rose: optical USB and optical Ethernet are different

HiFi Rose validates the practical use of pluggable optical interfaces in hi-fi equipment, but its ports must be distinguished. The documented RS130-to-RD160 connection uses USB 3.0 FIBER at the source and SFP AUDIO IN at the DAC, with RD160-DAC selected in the source’s output settings. This is a manufacturer-specific optical USB connection, not an open audio protocol carried by an Ethernet MAC. The RS130’s optical Ethernet networking function is a separate interface.9

Public manuals do not disclose enough framing detail to reverse-engineer that USB optical transport. Nor should every Rose link be described as 850 nm duplex-LC 1G SFP: the separate RSA720 optical USB hub manual specifies paired BiDi SFP+ optics. Its restrictions also preclude treating it as a universal bridge for every USB 2.0 DAC. O2AT therefore specifies its own optical baseline and Ethernet framing instead of inferring compatibility from a similar cage.

3.3 MSB Pro ISL and Aurender N50: the relevant lesson

Aurender documents an optional MSB Pro ISL output for the N50, with PCM through 768 kHz and native DSD512 among the module’s advertised capabilities. A native Pro ISL output permits a compatible MSB DAC connection without placing an external Pro USB adapter in that particular signal chain. This is evidence of cross-manufacturer implementation, but the public documentation does not establish the contractual details of FPGA-core licensing or universal compatibility with other modular Aurender products.10

MSB explicitly identifies Pro ISL as non-Ethernet. Its fibre guidance specifies matched single-mode optics and duplex LC fibre, including a 1310 nm module family, rather than establishing 850 nm 1000BASE-SX as the universal MSB implementation. The guide also distinguishes Pro ISL from other MSB optical links. An Ethernet switch is therefore not a compatible intermediary, and an arbitrary module should not be inserted into an MSB port merely because it fits the cage.11

MSB describes a return copy of the DAC master clock to the source, together with error handling. The important architectural lesson is receiver clock authority across an isolated connection. Public evidence does not disclose the exact serial line rate, frame format, 8b/10b use or internal FIFO topology. Those details must remain unspecified here. The Pro USB adapter is a USB-to-Pro-ISL path, not an obligatory component of every MSB digital input configuration.12

O2AT adopts the general benefits of optical isolation, buffered reception and explicit timing control, while retaining an ordinary Ethernet MAC and a published protocol. A raw proprietary FPGA serial bus and standard Layer-2 Ethernet can both use SFP hardware. That shared hardware does not make their frames or clocks interoperable.

4. O2AT Design Objectives & Architectural Principles

The replacement must be understandable to a listener and precise enough for two independent engineering teams to implement. A direct optical connection should discover its peer, agree a supported format, prefill a bounded buffer and start safely. It should not require the listener to choose clock-recovery constants, FPGA pins or a network addressing scheme.

4.2 Isolation, openness and a stable channel model

The fibre assembly shall contain no conductive path between the two O2AT ports. This isolates the signal link galvanically. It does not guarantee isolation of the entire installation when protective earth, USB, analogue interconnects or shared coaxial reference-clock distribution also connect chassis. Safety-earth arrangements must remain governed by the equipment design; the protocol cannot cancel an external conductive path.

PCM and DSD shall be transported without implicit volume control, sample-rate conversion, channel reordering or DoP insertion. Any processing belongs to an explicitly selected endpoint function outside the transparent transport mode. Two-channel ordering is left then right. Additional channels use a negotiated map with defined logical channel identifiers, replacing the undocumented clock/data/flag assignments associated with I²S over HDMI.

Reference RTL, packet encoders, dissectors, schematics and conformance vectors should be published under clear open licences. The proposed release policy is MIT for software/RTL and CERN-OHL-P v2 for hardware, with a separately stated open document licence for the specification. Third-party PHY IP and vendor tools retain their own licence conditions. No existing open reference implementation is claimed by this paper.

4.3 Replacing TOSLINK variants and allowing future ADAT

The target connection family includes rectangular F05 TOSLINK and 3.5 mm mini-TOSLINK. Those mechanical variants can carry the same optical consumer signal; changing their plug shape does not define a new audio coding system. O2AT replaces that connection with a negotiated duplex SFP link. Existing optical sources require an active bridge with the appropriate receiver and framing decoder, not a passive F05-to-LC adaptor.13

A complete migration must also distinguish programme types. Stereo PCM samples can map into the present PCM32 mode, and a recognised DoP stream can be unpacked to native DSD without changing its DSD bits. IEC 61937 encoded bursts and IEC 60958 channel-status, user and validity information require a separately defined legacy-carriage profile if transparent preservation is intended. Version 0.2 does not label those non-PCM bursts as ordinary PCM or promise their automatic decoding.14

5. Physical Layer & Wire Protocol Specification

5.1 Mandatory 1G optical baseline

The baseline shall implement an IEEE 802.3 Ethernet MAC with 1000BASE-X PCS/PMA at 1.25 GBd, full duplex. Its common optical profile is 1000BASE-SX at 850 nm using matched duplex-LC modules and OM3 or OM4 multimode glass fibre. Optional 1000BASE-LX single-mode links shall be identified explicitly and use qualified optics and fibre at both ends. A connector alone does not identify wavelength, launch power, receiver sensitivity or Ethernet compatibility.16

The source and receiver each contain a transmitter and a receiver. One fibre carries source-to-sink audio; the other carries discovery, control, credits and status in the opposite direction. Ethernet capacity is full duplex, so reverse control traffic does not consume the forward link’s serial bandwidth. MAC processing and buffers still require capacity for both directions.

A host shall implement module presence, transmit disable, loss-of-signal and fault handling, appropriate power sequencing and the required electrical interface. It should expose module identity and supported diagnostics. Only qualified module classes should be enabled. A 10G-only host cannot be presumed to operate a 1G SFP, and replacing an optic does not change the host PCS or SerDes rate. The baseline shall implement and enable 1000BASE-X auto-negotiation, advertising full duplex. Forced operation is an explicit paired-test option. Optional PAUSE capability shall be advertised and configured consistently; speed and duplex shall be reported.

The receiver reference design should begin with a low-power optical module, preferably a qualified part below 0.5 W, but this is an endpoint engineering target rather than a redefinition of an MSA power class. Coherent’s FTLF8519P3xyL provides a documented example below 500 mW. The complete budget must add the cage interface, FPGA transceiver, PCS/MAC, memories, regulators and clock circuitry.17

5.3 Ethernet envelope and identification

8b/10b constrains run lengths and bounds running disparity. It does not produce an exact 50% optical duty cycle in every symbol. Its purpose includes reliable serial recovery and AC-coupled channel behaviour; a laser is not physically unable to remain on or off for a long run. The 1G SerDes still performs clock recovery even when the pluggable module has no CDR.19

The packet path is audio packing, o2at header, Ethernet MAC, 1000BASE-X PCS/PMA and optical conversion. Standard destination and source MAC addresses remain present. There are no IP, UDP, RTP or TCP headers. The MAC supplies the normal Ethernet FCS, preamble, start delimiter and interpacket gap. The PCS supplies 8b/10b coding at 1G or 64b/66b at 10G. These are distinct layers, and the o2at encoder shall not add another private line-code layer.

Laboratory v0.2 frames use EtherType 0x88B5. This is a local experimental EtherType, not a permanent O2AT allocation. Its use shall remain inside the developing organisation’s controlled evaluation domain; a production release needs an appropriately assigned protocol identifier. The lowercase four-byte magic in every packet improves experimental demultiplexing but does not create ownership of the EtherType.20

The minimum interoperable Ethernet MTU is 1,500 bytes. Jumbo frames are not required. Audio payload is capped at 1,440 bytes in v0.2, leaving 48 bytes of unused MTU after the fixed header. Control payload may occupy up to 1,488 bytes. Packets shorter than the Ethernet minimum are padded by the MAC; padding is excluded from the o2at audio-group count and control-body length. Receivers shall validate the complete implied length before accepting data.

A single optional 802.1Q tag may use PCP 6 when the network administrator has mapped that priority appropriately. PCP is a scheduling request, not a reservation or latency guarantee. The default direct link is untagged. Do not send user audio to IEEE bridge-control multicast addresses such as 01:80:C2:00:00:2F. Discover with bounded broadcast traffic on an isolated evaluation link, then use unicast MAC addresses and explicit session admission.

A switch shall be qualified for forwarding the experimental EtherType, the selected VLAN, frame sizes, multicast or broadcast policy and required egress capacity. Network ACLs, port security, unknown-protocol filtering and energy-saving policies can affect operation. No EtherType value guarantees universal NIC driver exposure or switch forwarding. Raw-socket software must likewise be tested against the actual NIC and operating system.

Wire version 0x2 occupies the upper nibble of byte 4; it is not a standalone 0x02 byte. The format and channel configuration are immutable session context, established before audio. This header supersedes the inconsistent 18-byte layout in the attached v0.1 draft.

5.5 Control body, capabilities and session configuration

The format/rate/channel map cannot change merely because a new packet arrives. An unknown session, unsupported version, invalid group count, inconsistent length or wrong source binding is rejected before audio FIFO commitment. Ethernet padding is not audio. Frames not matching the magic, supported version and admitted session binding shall be discarded, including unrelated uses of experimental EtherType 0x88B5.

Every CONTROL body starts with a 16-byte prefix. Byte 0 is opcode; byte 1 is kind, with 0 request, 1 response and 2 notification. Bytes 2–3 are a status code, zero for success, requests and notifications. Bytes 4–7 hold a nonzero transaction_id for requests/responses and zero for notifications. Bytes 8–15 hold a source-selected 64-bit session_nonce, zero only during discovery. Prefix fields use big-endian encoding.

TLVs follow the prefix. Each has a uint16 type, uint16 value length, the value and zero padding so that the entire record is a multiple of eight bytes. Length excludes the four-byte TLV header and its padding. Type lookup uses type & 0x7fff; bit 15 marks a critical extension. Reject unknown critical TLVs, nonzero reserved fields, conflicting duplicate mandatory TLVs and any length extending beyond the declared body. Ignore well-formed unknown non-critical TLVs.

5.6 State machine and receiver credits

The control opcodes are 0x01 HELLO, 0x02 OPEN, 0x03 START, 0x04 END, 0x05 ABORT, 0x06 CREDIT, 0x07 PING and 0x08 STATUS. The receiver moves through IDLE, OPEN, ARMED, RUNNING and DRAINING, returning to IDLE after a controlled end or abort. Link loss or reset retires the session and enters a muted state. Discovery broadcasts should be limited to one per second after an initial bounded attempt; all established-session traffic is unicast.

HELLO uses session ID and nonce zero and returns capabilities. OPEN uses session ID zero with a fresh source nonce and all mandatory requested descriptors. A successful response assigns a nonzero session ID and echoes the complete accepted configuration and nonce. START acknowledges that context and returns initial credit_state. Audio shall not be sent before that acknowledgement. Initial credit shall be at least prefill_packets × G, within validated receiver storage. G shall be at least one and 4 × channels × G shall not exceed 1,440. The credit interval, path delay and source response time must fit the buffer window. Playback begins after the agreed prefill; a stream shorter than that threshold may start once its complete final range is buffered and FINAL_AUDIO plus END agree. A repeated START never resets a running FIFO or sequence counter.

Suggested response codes are 0 OK, 1 UNSUPPORTED_VERSION, 2 UNSUPPORTED_FORMAT, 3 UNSUPPORTED_RATE, 4 UNSUPPORTED_MAP, 5 BUSY, 6 BAD_STATE, 7 BAD_LENGTH, 8 UNKNOWN_SESSION, 9 BAD_TRANSACTION and 10 CAPACITY_EXCEEDED. Unknown major wire versions are discarded at framing; an understood control exchange may additionally report an unsupported requested version or capability.

Every regular AUDIO packet contains exactly negotiated G groups. FINAL_AUDIO contains 1…G groups and is the final packet, including when the final count equals G. The receiver verifies agreement, drains only that range and closes the session. An empty stream closes with total_groups = 0 and no audio packet. Cached END and ABORT responses remain available after retirement for at least the maximum retry duration plus admitted frame residence. After playing the last group of a validated FINAL_AUDIO packet, the receiver enters a drained, safely muted state and awaits END if it has not yet arrived. The receiver unwraps sequence16 into an extended packet index. For regular packets, the first group index equals extended_packet_index × G. Frames failing protocol or session validation are discarded without disturbing unrelated sessions. A corrupt Ethernet frame may cause a subsequent confirmed gap in the active stream. The active window must remain below 32,768 packets, and admitted frame residence must be shorter than 32,768 / packet_rate. The deployment shall be loop-free and bounded; a 16-bit sequence cannot distinguish arbitrarily old traffic circulating in a network loop.

5.7 PCM and native DSD packing

PCM uses signed two’s-complement 32-bit containers in network byte order. Valid 16- or 24-bit sample values occupy the most significant bits, with unused least significant bits zero. A group contains one container per channel in the accepted map order; groups follow increasing sample time. For 16-bit PCM, +1 is 00 01 00 00 and −1 is ff ff 00 00. For 24-bit PCM, +1 is 00 00 01 00 and −1 is ff ff ff 00. Full 32-bit values have no padding.

DSD32 is a packing format, not 32-bit PCM. Each channel word contains 32 successive one-bit samples, with the earliest sample in bit 31. Emit that word big-endian. The next word belongs to the next channel at the same 32-bit time block, followed by the next group. For a word 0x80000000, only the first one-bit sample is 1; for 0x40000001, the second and thirty-second are 1. No DoP markers, arithmetic scaling or implicit bit reversal are allowed.

“MSB-first” here describes the numerical octet order and the DSD chronological mapping after decoding. It does not override IEEE Ethernet’s bit transmission, scrambling or PCS rules. The MAC/PHY remains standards-compliant. Explicit decoded payload vectors are therefore more useful than describing the laser waveform as an audio word.

5.8 Packetisation and exact occupied-wire budget

Let C be channels and G be groups per packet. Both PCM32 and DSD32 use P = 4CG audio bytes. For a maximum-size audio packet, G = floor(1,440 / (4C)); P is then 1,440 bytes for 2 or 8 channels, and 1,408 bytes for 16 or 32. A 125 µs packetisation deadline additionally limits G to floor(group_rate × 125 µs), with a minimum of one. Use the smaller constraint and negotiate it once per session.

At lower rates, that deadline produces short regular packets rather than variable-duration regular packets. For example, stereo 48 kHz uses six groups and 48 audio bytes per packet. Every bandwidth claim shall specify G or the deadline that determines it. In the extreme-rate table, the maximum-size cap is reached before 125 µs, so all rows use the payload-alignment values above.

For ordinary audio packets here, occupied byte times are P + 50 untagged or P + 54 with one VLAN tag: 12 o2at header, 14 MAC header, optional 4 VLAN, 4 FCS, 8 preamble/SFD and 12 IFG. More generally, occupied byte times are 38 + max(46, 12 + P) untagged, or 42 + max(42, 12 + P) for a minimally padded single-tag frame. Ethernet padding therefore cannot be forgotten for very short packets.

PCM packet_rate = Fs / G. DSD packet_rate = FDSD / (32G). Occupied wire rate = 8 × occupied_byte_times × packet_rate. PCM audio rate = 32 × C × Fs; DSD audio rate = C × FDSD. Compare the occupied rate against 1.000 or 10.000 Gb/s MAC capacity. Do not deduct 8b/10b twice: it is already represented by the 1.25 GBd physical rate supplying the 1G MAC service.

The advanced direct-link ceiling is 85% of occupied wire capacity, including other traffic. Eight-channel 48-family extremes use 81.59232%, leaving 18.40768% physical capacity and 3.40768 percentage points below that policy. An 80% policy rejects this mode. The bandwidth budget alone does not establish a switched-latency bound.

6. Clocking Architecture, 10 MHz Discipline & Flow Control

6.1 Separate serial recovery from conversion timing

An SFP receiver and host SerDes recover enough serial timing to identify symbols and bytes. That recovered clock is necessary for transport, but it shall not directly clock the D/A conversion process in any O2AT receiver. The reference tier additionally requires external-reference hardware. Accepted packets enter a dual-clock FIFO. The write side belongs to the MAC or local transport domain; the read side belongs to the audio domain. Packet-delay variation changes FIFO occupancy while the conversion cadence remains controlled locally. The Ethernet SerDes and MAC clocks shall independently satisfy their Ethernet timing requirements; disciplining the audio clock does not imply that Ethernet or USB signalling clocks are controlled by the 10 MHz input.

This architecture removes a direct timing transfer path. It does not make all interface effects disappear. Supply noise from the optical receiver can still modulate an oscillator, inappropriate clock-domain crossings can corrupt data, and inadequate buffering can cause discontinuities. A defensible claim is that packet arrival is not the conversion-clock reference, subject to a validated buffer and clock-control design. An unqualified claim of zero interface jitter or automatically superior sound is not warranted.

The optical receive clock supports byte recovery and FIFO writes. The audio clock supports FIFO reads and the converter interface. Feedback controls delivery from the source; it does not substitute packet arrival for the conversion reference.

6.2 Why the reference tier includes a 10 MHz input

A shared reference can make the source and sink sample-rate generators frequency coherent. This is useful when a source has a real-time acquisition or DSP clock that cannot be freely paused, when several receivers must avoid long-term differential drift, or when a system already distributes a high-quality reference. It can remove the need for a network-time servo in a dedicated installation. For that reason, an external 10 MHz input is mandatory hardware on an o2at-reference receiver and optional on o2at-basic.

The benefit is conditional on the actual audio clocks being derived from that reference with the intended rational ratios. Merely connecting two BNC sockets to the same oscillator does not prove that their audio paths are disciplined. A common reference suppresses relative frequency error in a properly locked design; it does not by itself align divider phase, sample number, stream start or DAC group delay. Different startup phases can leave a fixed offset even when long-term rates agree.

The earlier phrase “zero-ppm-drift synchronous FIFO operation” is therefore an idealised frequency result, not a complete synchronisation method. Real lock acquisition, reference faults, holdover drift and servo behaviour require measurement and status reporting. Multiple DACs needing sample-accurate acoustic alignment require an explicit epoch or trigger mechanism and calibration beyond v0.2’s consumer link profile. PTP or another time-distribution mechanism remains appropriate where network-wide time is actually required.

Frequency accuracy and short-term phase noise are also different specifications. A highly accurate 10 MHz source can produce a worse audio clock after an unsuitable synthesiser than a good local oscillator. The reference design should compare phase-noise spectra and integrated jitter over stated offset bands in internal and external modes, with repeatable conditions. A narrow reference-tracking loop and a suitable local oscillator may preserve short-term noise performance while following the long-term reference.

6.3 Clock-input and synthesiser engineering

The proposed reference input is a labelled 50 Ω BNC port. Its baseline qualification shall include a 10 MHz sine input at 0.5 V rms into 50 Ω, approximately +7 dBm, and an AC-coupled 50% square input of 2 V peak-to-peak at the termination. The square-wave qualification is zero-mean at the terminated input, with no DC offset applied. The manufacturer shall state accepted amplitude ranges, input coupling, absolute limits and lock thresholds. A 50 Ω port must not silently be specified as 75 Ω, and voltage, power and waveform conventions must be explicit.

The front end should use appropriate termination, filtering, protection and a low-additive-noise conditioning stage. It shall detect missing or out-of-range reference input. Entering or leaving external lock shall trigger a controlled transition: report status, stop or drain the stream where necessary, mute safely, reacquire and restart. Switching an audio clock source while the converter is live is not an acceptable substitute for a transition design.

Si5341 and LMK04828 are examples requiring different clock plans, not interchangeable generic fractional-N audio solutions. Si5341 is a flexible clock generator using fractional MultiSynth synthesis; its presence alone does not establish that it attenuates input jitter as required. LMK04828 is a dual-loop jitter-cleaner/distribution device whose oscillator, PLL and divider constraints must be checked against the desired audio rates. The usual LMK04828 dual-loop cleaning plan also requires an external VCXO or tunable crystal. Neither part should be selected from its headline jitter number without analysing the full reference-to-output transfer.21

The 44.1 and 48 kHz families shall have explicit frequency plans, including reference division, oscillator choice, output division, spur locations, lock time and output interface. At the new extreme rates, familiar 256 × Fs assumptions are often inappropriate. Use the converter’s supported clock architecture rather than multiplying a legacy ratio until it exceeds the device’s limits. FPGA PLLs can serve PCS and internal logic, while the conversion clock should be provided by a measured clock subsystem appropriate to the DAC.

A shared coaxial clock distributor may reconnect chassis electrically through BNC shields even when the audio path is optical. Therefore, O2AT guarantees a non-conductive audio-link path, not unconditional whole-system galvanic isolation. A reference installation must account for those parallel paths and any isolation implemented by the clock inputs or distribution equipment.

6.5 Buffer sizing, latency and loss behaviour

Buffer sizes shall be expressed in bytes and audio groups with an associated format, not simply in “samples”. At stereo PCM32/3,072 kHz, 16,384 sample groups occupy 131,072 bytes and represent 5.333 ms. Eight channels require 524,288 bytes for the same group count. For DSD, a group spans 32 one-bit times per channel, so the group rate is the DSD bit rate divided by 32.

For an uncompensated relative rate error ε, the approximate time to consume an available FIFO margin of M groups is M / (group_rate × |ε|). As an illustration, a 1 ms margin with 50 ppm mismatch lasts about 20 s. A large FIFO postpones a steady-state problem; it does not eliminate it. Margin must also cover in-flight packets, source response time, processing stalls and any admitted switch bursts.

An initial reference design may target 0.5–2 ms of FIFO occupancy, but this is a prototype operating range, not a universal latency promise. Define the packetisation deadline, the largest allowed burst, the credit update interval and the worst-case control round trip, then size free space above the high watermark accordingly. Credits shall count data already sent but not yet consumed so that outstanding packets cannot overrun the receiver.

The latency budget includes packet assembly, serialisation, fibre propagation, switch forwarding/queueing if present, FIFO residence and the converter interface. A full 1,440-byte audio packet occupies 11.952 µs of tagged 1G wire time including IFG. That is only one component of the path. At low rates, a 125 µs packetisation deadline avoids waiting 3.75 ms to fill a stereo 48 kHz packet. Shorter packets trade bandwidth efficiency for assembly latency.

Ethernet FCS detects frame corruption; it is not forward error correction and there is no mandatory retransmission of late audio. Frames failing FCS or syntax validation are discarded and counted. A confirmed gap, invalid active-stream state or underrun shall invoke that stream’s defined mute/restart procedure; unrelated malformed or foreign traffic shall not mute an admitted stream. PCM may be ramped towards zero by an explicitly identified output-protection stage. Raw DSD shall not be replaced blindly by an all-zero bitstream, which represents a large DC condition in the one-bit domain. Use converter mute or a validated DSD idle/transition sequence.

7. Technical Challenges & Reference Implementation

7.1 Power, RF coupling and clock-domain crossings

Place the optical cage and high-speed PHY circuitry away from the lowest-noise oscillator, DAC reference and analogue output paths. Use a controlled return-current structure, appropriate cage bonding, local regulation and filtering, adequate thermal paths and a layout validated for the actual serial channel. Galvanic isolation between boxes does not isolate a DAC’s local analogue circuitry from its own SFP supply current.

Select optics by supported rate, optical budget, qualification, diagnostics and measured electrical behaviour. A non-retimed low-power module can be a sensible reference choice, but CDR absence is not a universal conformance requirement. Faster optics do not necessarily corrupt conversion timing, and a nominally slower optic is not automatically quieter. Test supply spectra and analogue output under realistic payload, burst, idle, module temperature and cable-loss conditions.

A dual-clock FIFO should use independently clocked read and write pointers, Gray-coded pointer transfer, synchronisers with a documented metastability analysis, and correctly constrained reset release. Do not synchronise a changing binary multi-bit counter by placing two flip-flops on each bit. Data storage, metadata and format changes must cross as a coherent transaction. Vendor FIFO IP can help, but its configuration and timing constraints still require review.

The receive path shall not release an audio packet to the playback FIFO until the complete frame has passed FCS and syntax checks. Either buffer the whole frame before committing it or support an atomic rollback that never exposes corrupt groups. A late CRC result cannot undo samples already converted. Likewise, a format change shall drain or discard the old stream under mute before a new session is admitted.

7.2 FPGA and host reference design

A practical first platform is Sipeed’s Tang Mega 138K Pro, which provides two SFP+ cages and a GW5AST-LV138FPG676A device with transceivers spanning rates that include 1.25 Gb/s. The baseline experiment uses a qualified 1G optic in a compatible cage and configures the FPGA accordingly. It does not require operating the optical link at 10G merely because the board has SFP+ cages.23

The project must implement or integrate the 1000BASE-X PCS/PMA, MAC, receive validation, control-state machine, audio packing, dual-clock FIFOs and output interface. Verify the available transceiver reference clocks and rate configurations before board commitment. The board’s commercial Gowin toolchain requirement and silicon-revision restrictions must be recorded. Open RTL does not imply a fully open implementation toolchain or a ready-made, validated O2AT bitstream.

A host prototype can send raw Ethernet frames through a suitable NIC, but an AF_PACKET socket does not automatically provide zero-copy, bounded scheduling latency or reliable PHY control. Measure the actual path, use appropriate batching or ring buffers where supported, and keep audio buffering and receiver credits visible. The release should publish a packet dissector, a reference encoder/decoder, deterministic test vectors and reproducible builds before attempting broad hardware optimisation.

The optional expansion profile uses 10GBASE-R at 10.3125 GBd and a 10.000 Gb/s MAC service. Qualified 10GBASE-SR or LR media are allowed under an explicit profile. A mixed-speed switch can bridge a 10G source to a 1G DAC port only after admission to the slower egress. Mixed-speed paths shall also bound source or upstream-switch bursts to the qualified egress service and queue capacity. Average admission and receiver credits alone do not protect an intermediate switch from a rapid burst at 10G. The protocol remains raw Layer 2; no IP workload is required to justify a 10G link.

7.3 USB ingress and DAC egress impose separate ceilings

The conventional high-speed USB 2.0 high-bandwidth isochronous endpoint limit is three transactions of up to 1,024 bytes per 125 µs microframe. The maximum payload is therefore 24.576 MB/s, or 196.608 Mb/s. Stereo PCM32/3,072 kHz requires exactly that nominal payload, leaving no positive rate margin for asynchronous clock error. A 385-sample-group microframe would need 3,080 bytes. The 44.1 kHz-family extreme fits the theoretical three-transaction budget, but endpoint and driver support remain separate requirements.24

A USB-to-o2at bridge also needs a real feedback controller. Ethernet credits prevent receive-buffer overrun, but do not by themselves specify UAC2 feedback. Estimate downstream consumption from cumulative DAC-side group counters against the bridge’s observed USB microframe count, filter bounded report-delay variation and control source-buffer occupancy. Define the conversion from o2at groups to the advertised USB sample units; PCM and vendor-specific native-DSD endpoints need different mappings. Verify loop stability, startup, positive/negative clock offsets and feedback quantisation. Packet interarrival variation shall not become the DAC conversion-clock reference.

Generic “XMOS UAC2” is not enough evidence for these rates. As a concrete documented implementation limit, XMOS lib_xud v4.0.1 supports at most two transactions per microframe, on the specified xcore.ai configuration. That yields 16.384 MB/s and falls short of stereo DSD2048 or PCM32 at 2,822.4/3,072 kHz. This is a limit of the cited implementation, not a statement about every possible future XMOS design.25

Begin extreme-rate transport validation with a native FPGA pattern generator or a separately qualified high-throughput ingress. A suitable USB 3 implementation is a possible later bridge. The intended user-facing format must be supported by the source software, host driver, endpoint descriptors, device firmware and downstream interface. A successful optical packet test is not evidence that a present-day music server can generate the same format.

At the DAC side, two 32-bit I²S slots require BCLK = 64 × Fs. PCM32/2,822.4 kHz therefore needs 180.6336 MHz BCLK, and PCM32/3,072 kHz needs 196.608 MHz. One serial DSD line per channel needs 90.3168 or 98.304 MHz at the extreme rates. These are calculations from the stated packing, not guaranteed operating modes of existing I²S-over-HDMI links. Multiple internal lanes or a converter-specific interface may be necessary.26

7.4 Applying the architecture to the N30SA, µDDC and Harmony

Aurender explicitly states that an external clock input on its source does not discipline asynchronous USB audio; the downstream audio device controls average data flow. Thus, connecting an N30SA by USB to a µDDC does not make the µDDC the N30SA’s USB signalling-clock generator. In asynchronous USB operation, the sink’s feedback controls the host’s average delivery of audio samples. The µDDC’s internal relationship between that feedback and its external reference remains undocumented in the public specifications reviewed here. The N30SA’s reference-clock treatment of AES/S/P-DIF is a different path.27

LAiV specifies a 10 MHz, 50 Ω clock input for the Harmony µDDC, accepting square or sine waves, and lists PCM through 768 kHz and DSD512 on its USB/I²S paths. Those specifications do not establish support for DSD2048 or PCM3072, nor do they disclose every internal feedback and conversion-clock relationship. The original Harmony DAC in this experiment has no 10 MHz input, so reference discipline would be applied at the intermediate interface rather than directly at that DAC.28

The useful first experiment is therefore a transparent bridge within the existing equipment’s supported formats: asynchronous USB ingress, o2at optical transport, a receiver with explicit local or disciplined read clock, and a validated µDDC/DAC-compatible output. Confirm where the downstream conversion clock is actually selected. Separate transport stress tests at DSD2048/PCM3072 can then demonstrate future capacity without implying that the current playback chain accepts those rates.

9. Industry Adoption, Validation & Governance

9.1 Why adoption will be gradual

Manufacturers already have substantial investment in USB receivers, XMOS firmware, S/P-DIF input circuits, certification, drivers and support processes. Those are working product assets. A new optical socket adds a cage, an Ethernet-capable digital path, firmware, power and thermal requirements, plus another interoperability matrix. Conservative adoption is a rational response to those costs, especially while compatible sources are scarce.

Consumers recognise TOSLINK and USB and can connect them with inexpensive cables. O2AT must therefore win on clear practical benefits: isolation, a documented format envelope, robust negotiation, replaceable standard optics and dependable operation. Extreme sample rates provide design headroom, but do not by themselves establish a listening benefit or persuade a manufacturer to redesign a successful DAC.

Boutique partnerships can create the first compatible source module, receiver board and reference DAC input. A small common baseline is more useful than several incompatible demonstrations. Open reference RTL, schematics, conformance traces and an accessible analyser give independent engineers a way to reproduce behaviour and identify defects without relying on brand-specific claims.

9.2 A measurable validation programme

  • Protocol: publish exact PCM and DSD test vectors; validate packing, channel maps, sequence wrap, session restart, malformed lengths, unknown critical TLVs and experimental EtherType coexistence.
  • Link and thermal: test qualified optics over the declared loss and temperature range, including module replacement, loss of signal, fault recovery, rail transients and sustained maximum-rate traffic.
  • Rate and buffering: run both frequency families, stereo and eight-channel extremes, bursty hosts, bounded switch contention, controlled ppm offsets and clock-source changes. Log long-term FIFO occupancy and grants.
  • Clock and analogue: measure phase-noise spectra and integrated jitter with stated bandwidths; compare converter output under internal/external reference, link idle/full traffic and alternative qualified modules.
  • Interoperability: demonstrate two independent source implementations and two independent receivers, plus a documented switch configuration for the optional fabric profile.
  • Failure behaviour: inject missing, duplicated, stale, out-of-order and corrupted frames; verify counters, bounded reaction, safe mute and recovery without unintended full-scale PCM or DSD output.

For an illustrative error-rate check, zero observed errors over N independent tested bits gives an approximate one-sided 95% upper bound of 3/N on the bit-error probability. A 24-hour test at 1.25 Gbit/s exercises about 1.08 × 10¹⁴ serial bits, giving an illustrative bound near 2.8 × 10⁻¹⁴ under that model. A MAC-only payload test measures a different population and should be labelled accordingly. Neither result proves zero future errors or audible superiority.

Likewise, a hypothetical BER of 10⁻¹² at 1 Gbit/s corresponds to a mean raw-bit error interval of about 1,000 s, not eleven days. Packet error statistics, detected/rejected frames and actual audio interruption rates are distinct quantities. Publish exposure time, tested bit population, confidence model and failure policy rather than a single reassuring number.

9.3 Release gates and future extensions

Version 0.2 should remain an engineering draft until the packet/control definitions have two independent implementations, the baseline optical profile has qualified parts and the conformance suite exposes no unresolved safety-of-output failures. Public commercial deployment also requires a proper protocol identifier. A governance group should manage the capability registry, change process, errata and reference releases with public decisions.

Future work may add sample-accurate multi-DAC epochs, redundant paths, authenticated control, scheduled Ethernet operation and broader sample formats. These should use negotiated extensions and explicit version changes where necessary. The mandatory consumer baseline should remain small enough to implement without a full IP stack, PTP servo or proprietary licence. The standard’s success should be measured by interchangeable endpoints and reliable playback, not by its longest list of optional features.

Appendix A. Commercial Pushback from the Audiophile Cable Industry

A commodity optical interface challenges the economics of premium metallic digital interconnects. The original proposition compared USB, AES/EBU, S/P-DIF and silver I²S-HDMI cables priced at US$500 to US$5,000 or more with an US$8 duplex-LC patch cord and US$15 SFP modules. In this revision those figures are illustrative commercial assumptions, not verified current offers or a guaranteed system bill of materials. Two endpoints, their optics, electronics, integration and compliance work must all be counted.

A.1 What commodity fibre changes physically

A non-conductive glass-fibre signal path cannot carry a chassis-to-chassis ground current along the fibre. A continuous metallic conductor can. Silver purity, conductor geometry and shielding do not turn that conductor into glass. However, transformer-coupled electrical interfaces and active isolation products can provide galvanic barriers of their own, and ordinary TOSLINK already provides optical isolation. The defensible comparison is the complete interface topology, not a claim that every expensive cable is equivalent or every copper-based system lacks isolation.

A correctly specified inexpensive optical patch cord can therefore deliver the relevant isolation property without precious-metal conductors. Its quality still matters for insertion loss, connector polish, mechanical durability, bend handling and reliable mating. O2AT does not eliminate engineering quality; it relocates much of the relevant performance from luxury conductor construction to standard optical specifications and endpoint design.

A.2 Likely incentives, not an allegation of coordinated resistance

Some firms whose margins depend on premium metallic digital cables may have little incentive to promote an interchangeable optical connection. Others may sell qualified fibre assemblies, rugged connectors, installation services, optical diagnostics or complete interface products. These are plausible commercial responses, not evidence of a coordinated campaign by any manufacturer. No particular firm’s motives can be established from its product price.

The strongest adoption argument is an open and measurable interface. Demonstrate bit integrity, controlled failure behaviour, real isolation boundaries, reproducible clock measurements and an honest complete cost. Listening comparisons can supplement that evidence when appropriately controlled, but they cannot substitute for the transport specification or prove that an expensive digital cable is necessary for a correctly functioning link.
